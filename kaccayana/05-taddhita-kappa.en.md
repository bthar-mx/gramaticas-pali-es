**350\. 373\. Yena vā saṃsaṭṭhaṃ tarati carati vahati ṇiko (764).** \[Yena \+ vā \+ saṃsaṭṭhaṃ \+ tarati \+ carati \+ vahati \+ ṇiko, 7\]

Yena vā saṃsaṭṭhaṃ, yena vā tarati, yena vā carati, yena vā vahati icc etesv atthesu **ṇika**paccayo hoti vā.

Tilena saṃsaṭṭhaṃ bhojanaṃ telikaṃ, tilena saṃsaṭṭhaṃ vā. Evaṃ goḷikaṃ, ghātikaṃ.

**Vā ti vikappanatthena** aññesu pi **ṇika**paccayo hoti. Rājagahe vasatī ti rājagahiko, rājagahe vasati vā. Rājagahe jāto rājagahiko, rājagahe jāto vā. Evaṃ māgadhiko (M. i, 287), sāvatthiko, kapilavatthiko, pāṭaliputtiko, vesāliko (Vin. i, 27; A. iii, 47).

---

[There is] ‘ṇika’ [in these meanings]: mixed with that, he crosses by that, he goes by that, he carries by that.

Optionally, there is the suffix ‘ṇika’ in these meanings: “mixed with that (yena saṃsaṭṭhaṃ)”, “he crosses by that (yena tarati)”, “he goes by that (yena carati)”, “he carries by that (yena vahati)”.

---

Examples:

1. Tilena saṃsaṭṭhaṃ bhojanaṃ: **telikaṃ** (food mixed with sesamum seeds).
2. Guḷena saṃsaṭṭhaṃ bhojanaṃ: **goḷikaṃ** (food mixed with molasses).
3. Ghatena saṃsaṭṭhaṃ bhojanaṃ: **ghātikaṃ** (food mixed with ghee).

5. Uḷumpena taratī ti **oḷumpiko** (he crosses by raft: a rafter).
6. Sakaṭena caratī ti **sākaṭiko** (he goes by cart: a carter).
7. Pattena caratī ti **pattiko** (he goes by foot: a pedestrian).

9. Dhammena caratī ti **dhammiko** (he lives by the Dhamma: righteous).

12. Aṃsena vahatī ti **aṃsiko** (he carries with the shoulder: a shoulder-carrier).
13. Khandhena vahatī ti **khandhiko** (he carries with the shoulder: a shoulder-carrier).
14. Aṅgulīhi vahatī ti **aṅguliko** (he carries with the fingers).

“Vā” has the meaning of “alternative” (vikappana); in other [meanings] also there is the suffix ‘ṇika’.

Ekena vibhāgena ekadhā. Evaṃ dvidhā (Khu. vii, 179), tidhā (DhA. i, 171), catudhā (D. iii, 153), pañcadhā (AbhA. i, 385), chadhā.

**C**eti kimatthaṃ? **So**paccayo hoti. Suttaso (A. ii, 207), byañjanaso (A. i, 479), padaso (Vin. ii, 25).

---

And [there is] ‘dhā’ in division.

And in the meaning of division (vibhāga), there is the suffix ‘dhā’.

---

Examples:

1. Ekena vibhāgena **ekadhā** (division by one: in one way).

Likewise:

1. Dvīhi vibhāgena **dvidhā** (division by two: in two ways).
2. Tīhi vibhāgena **tidhā** (division by three: in three ways).
3. Catūhi vibhāgena **catudhā** (division by four: in four ways).
4. Pañcahi vibhāgena **pañcadhā** (division by five: in five ways).
5. Chahi vibhāgena **chadhā** (division by six: in six ways).

What is the purpose of saying “ca”? [To include that also] there is the suffix ‘so’.

Examples:

1. **Suttaso** (by sutta), **byañjanaso** (by letter), **padaso** (by word).

**398\. 421\. Sabbanāmehi pakāravacane tu thā (844).** \[Sabbanāmehi \+ pakāravacane \+ tu \+ thā, 4\]

Sabbanāmehi pakāravacanatthe **thā**paccayo hoti.

So pakāro tathā, taṃ pakāraṃ tathā, tena pakārena tathā, tassa pakārassa tathā, tasmā pakārā tathā, tassa pakārassa tathā, tasmiṃ pakāre tathā (M. i, 157; ii, 23). Evaṃ yathā (M. i, 157; ii, 23), sabbathā (M. ii, 27), aññathā (Vin. i, 116), itarathā (VinA. i, 230).

**Tuggahaṇaṃ** kimatthaṃ? **Thatthā[^38]** paccayo hoti. So pakāro tathatthā.[^39] Evaṃ yathatthā, sabbathatthā, aññathatthā, itarathatthā.

---

But after the pronouns, [there is] ‘thā’ in the expression of manner.

After the pronouns (sabbanāma), in the meaning of the expression of manner (pakāravacana), there is the suffix ‘thā’.

---

Examples:

1. So pakāro **tathā** (that manner: thus); taṃ pakāraṃ **tathā** (to that manner: thus); tena pakārena **tathā** (by that manner: thus); tassa pakārassa **tathā** (for that manner: thus); tasmā pakārā **tathā** (from that manner: thus); tassa pakārassa **tathā** (of that manner: thus); tasmiṃ pakāre **tathā** (in that manner: thus).

Likewise:

1. **yathā** (as).
2. **sabbathā** (in every manner).
3. **aññathā** (in another manner).
4. **itarathā** (in the other manner).

What is the purpose of taking ‘tu’? [To include] the suffix ‘thatthā’.

Examples:

1. So pakāro **tathatthā** (that manner). Likewise: **yathatthā**, **sabbathatthā**, **aññathatthā**, **itarathatthā**.

**399\. 422\. Kim-imehi thaṃ (845).** \[Kiṃ-imehi \+ thaṃ, 2\]

**Kiṃ ima** icc etehi **thaṃ**paccayo hoti pakāravacanatthe.

Ko pakāro kathaṃ (Khu. vi, 149), kaṃ pakāraṃ kathaṃ, kena pakārena kathaṃ, kassa pakārassa kathaṃ, kasmā pakārā kathaṃ, kassa pakārassa kathaṃ, kasmiṃ pakāre kathaṃ (Khu. vi, 149). Ayaṃ pakāro itthaṃ (D. i, 31), imaṃ pakāraṃ itthaṃ, iminā pakārena itthaṃ, imassa pakārassa itthaṃ, imasmā pakārā itthaṃ, imassa pakārassa itthaṃ, imasmiṃ pakāre itthaṃ (D. i, 31).

---

After “kim” and “ima”, [there is] ‘thaṃ’.

In the meaning of the expression of manner, after “kim” (what) and “ima” (this), there is the suffix ‘thaṃ’.

---

Examples:

1. Ko pakāro **kathaṃ** (what manner?: how?); kaṃ pakāraṃ **kathaṃ** (what manner?: how?); kena pakārena **kathaṃ** (by what manner?: how?); kassa pakārassa **kathaṃ** (for what manner?: how?); kasmā pakārā **kathaṃ** (from what manner?: how?); kassa pakārassa **kathaṃ** (of what manner?: how?); kasmiṃ pakāre **kathaṃ** (in what manner?: how?).
2. Ayaṃ pakāro **itthaṃ** (this manner: in this manner); imaṃ pakāraṃ **itthaṃ**; iminā pakārena **itthaṃ**; imassa pakārassa **itthaṃ**; imasmā pakārā **itthaṃ**; imassa pakārassa **itthaṃ**; imasmiṃ pakāre **itthaṃ**.

**400\. 364\. Vuddh’ ādisarassa vā ’saṃyogantassa saṇe ca (847).** \[Vuddhi \+ ādisarassa \+ vā \+ a-saṃyoga-antassa \+ saṇe \+ ca, 6\]

Ādisarassa vā asaṃyogantassa ādibyañjanassa vā sarassa vuddhi hoti saṇakārake paccaye pare.

Ābhidhammiko (AbhA. i, 12; JA. i, 234), Venateyyo (Khu. v, 204), Vāsiṭṭho (Vin ii, 11), ālasyaṃ (A. iii, 364), ārogyaṃ (D. i, 69).

**Asaṃyogantasse** ti kimatthaṃ? Bhaggavo (M. ii, 242; iii, 281; JA. ii, 73), Manteyyo, Kunteyyo.

---

And when there is [a suffix] with the ‘ṇ’ [indicatory letter], [there is] strengthening of the initial vowel that does not end in a conjunct consonant.

When a suffix with the [indicatory] letter ‘ṇ’ follows, there is strengthening of the initial vowel that does not end in a conjunct consonant, or of the vowel of the initial consonant.

---

Examples:

1. Abhidhammaṃ adhīte: **Ābhidhammiko** (he studies the Abhidhamma: one who studies the Abhidhamma).
2. Vinatāya apaccaṃ: **Venateyyo** (the offspring of Vinatā).
3. Vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṃ: **Vāsiṭṭho** (the offspring of Vasiṭṭha).
4. Alasassa bhāvo: **ālasyaṃ** (the state of the lazy one: laziness).
5. Arogassa bhāvo: **ārogyaṃ** (the state of absence of disease: health).

What is the purpose of saying “asaṃyogantassa” (that does not end in a conjunct consonant)? [To prevent the operation of this rule in cases like]: Bhaggussa apaccaṃ: **Bhaggavo** (the offspring of Bhaggu), Mantiyā apaccaṃ: **Manteyyo** (the offspring of Manti), Kuntiyā apaccaṃ: **Kunteyyo** (the offspring of Kunti), [where there is a conjunct consonant].

**401\. 375\. Mā y-ūnam āgamo ṭhāne (848).** \[Mā \+ i-ū-naṃ \+ āgamo \+ ṭhāne, 4\]

**I u** icc etesaṃ ādibhūtānaṃ mā vuddhi hoti, tesu ca **e-o**vuddhāgamo hoti ṭhāne.

Byākaraṇam adhīte veyyākaraṇiko (D. i, 82; DA. i, 220-Sad. sutta 850), nyāyam adhīte neyyāyiko, Byāvacchassa apaccaṃ Beyyāvaccho,[^40] dvāre niyutto dovāriko (A. ii, 481-Sad. sutta 854).

---

Let there be no [strengthening] of ‘i’ and ‘u’; in the [appropriate] place [there is] the insertion.[^41]

Let there be no strengthening of ‘i’ and ‘u’ when they are at the beginning; and in those [cases], in the appropriate place, there is the insertion of the strengthenings ‘e’ and ‘o’.

---

Examples:

1. Byākaraṇam adhīte **veyyākaraṇiko** (he studies grammar: a grammarian).
2. Nyāyam adhīte **neyyāyiko** (he studies logic: a logician).
3. Byāvacchassa apaccaṃ **Beyyāvaccho** (the offspring of Byāvaccha).
4. Dvāre niyutto **dovāriko** (occupied at the door: a gatekeeper).

**402\. 377\. Āttañ ca (857).** \[Āttaṃ \+ ca, 2\]

**I u** icc etesaṃ **ā**ttañ ca hoti, **ri**kārāgamo ca ṭhāne.

Isissa bhāvo ārisyaṃ,[^42] iṇassa bhāvo āṇyaṃ, usabhassa bhāvo āsabhaṃ (S. i, 265), ujuno bhāvo ajjavaṃ[^43] (A. i, 93), icc evamādī yojetabbā.

**Yūnam** iti kimatthaṃ? Apāyesu jāto āpāyiko (Khu. i, 203).

**Ṭhāne** ti kimatthaṃ? Vematiko (Vin. iv, 142), opanayiko,[^44] opamāyiko, opāyiko (Vin. iii, 57).

---

And [there is] ‘ā’.

And there is ‘ā’ of ‘i’ and ‘u’, and in the [appropriate] place there is the insertion of the [syllable] ‘ri’.

---

Examples:

1. Isissa bhāvo **ārisyaṃ** (the state of the sage: sageness).
2. Iṇassa bhāvo **āṇyaṃ** (the state of debt: indebtedness).
3. Usabhassa bhāvo **āsabhaṃ** (the state of the bull: bullness).
4. Ujuno bhāvo **ajjavaṃ** (the state of the straight: rectitude).

And so the remaining [cases] are to be formed.

What is the purpose of saying “yūnaṃ” (of ‘i’ and ‘u’)? [To prevent the operation of this rule in cases like]: apāyesu jāto **āpāyiko** (born in hell), where there are no initial ‘i’ or ‘u’.

What is the purpose of saying “ṭhāne” (in the appropriate place)? [To prevent the operation of this rule in cases like]: vimatiyā niyutto: **vematiko** (occupied with doubt: one who has doubt), upanaye niyutto: **opanayiko** (occupied in bringing near), upamāya niyutto: **opamāyiko** (occupied with a simile), upāye niyutto: **opāyiko** (occupied with an expedient), [where it is not the appropriate place].

**403\. 354\. Kvac’ ādi-majjh’-uttarānaṃ dīgha-rassā paccayesu ca (858).** \[Kvaci \+ ādi-majjha-uttarānaṃ \+ dīgha-rassā \+ paccayesu \+ ca, 5\]

Kvaci ādi-majjha-uttara icc etesaṃ dīgha-rassā honti paccayesu ca apaccayesu ca.

**Ādidīgho** tāva: Pākāro, nīvāro, pāsādo, pākaṭo, pātimokkho, pāṭikaṅkho icc evamādi.

**Majjhedīgho** tāva: Aṅgamāgadhiko, orabbhamāgaviko icc evamādi.

**Uttaradīgho** tāva: Khantī paramaṃ tapo titikkhā (Khu. i, 41), Añjanāgiri (JA. vii, 162), Koṭarāvanaṃ, aṅgulī icc evamādi.

**Ādirasso** tāva: Pageva (M. i, 188) icc evamādi.

**Majjherasso** tāva: Sumedhaso (Khu. i, 17) suvaṇṇadharehi[^45] icc evamādi.

**Uttararasso** tāva: Bhovādi nāma so hoti (Khu. i, 70), yathābhāvi guṇena so icc evamādi. Aññe pi yathājinavacanānuparodhena yojetabbā.

**Caggahaṇena** apaccayesu cā ti atthaṃ samucceti.

---

And sometimes, when suffixes follow, the initial, middle and final [vowels] [become] long or short.

Whether suffixes follow or do not follow, sometimes the initial, middle and final [vowels] become long or short.

First, **lengthening of the initial** (ādidīgha): **pākāro** (surrounding wall), **nīvāro** (a kind of grain), **pāsādo** (mansion), **pākaṭo** (famous), **pātimokkho**, **pāṭikaṅkho** (something to be desired or expected), etc.

**Lengthening of the middle** (majjhedīgha): **Aṅgamāgadhiko** (belonging to Aṅga and Magadha), **orabbhamāgaviko** (a hunter of rams), etc.

**Lengthening of the final** (uttaradīgha): **Khantī** paramaṃ tapo titikkhā (patience is the foremost ascetic practice), **Añjanāgiri** (the Añjanā mountain), **Koṭarāvanaṃ** (the Koṭarā forest), **aṅgulī** (finger), etc.

**Shortening of the initial** (ādirassa): **pageva** (much more), etc.

**Shortening of the middle** (majjherassa): **sumedhaso** (wise), **suvaṇṇadharehi** (with torrents of gold), etc.

**Shortening of the final** (uttararassa): **Bhovādi** nāma so hoti (he is called Bhovādi, a brahmin), yathābhāvi guṇena so (he is [called] Yathābhāvi by virtue), etc. The other [cases] are also to be formed without contradicting the Word of the Victorious One.

By taking “ca”, the meaning “also without suffixes (apaccayesu ca)” is collected.

**404\. 370\. Tesu vuddhi-lopāgama-vikāra-viparītādesā ca (859).** \[Tesu \+ vuddhi-lopa-āgama-vikāra-viparīta-ādesā \+ ca, 3\]

Tesu ādi-majjh’-uttaresu yathājinavacanānuparodhena[^46] kvaci vuddhi hoti, kvaci lopo hoti, kvaci āgamo hoti, kvaci vikāro hoti, kvaci viparīto hoti, kvaci ādeso hoti.

**Ādivuddhi** tāva: Ābhidhammiko, Venateyyo icc evamādi.

**Majjhevuddhi** tāva: Sukhaseyyaṃ, sukhakāri dānaṃ, sukhakāri sīlaṃ icc evamādi.

**Uttaravuddhi** tāva: Kāliṅgo (JA. iv, 233), Māgadhiko (M. i, 287), paccakkhadhammā icc evamādi.

**Ādilopo** tāva: Tālīsaṃ icc evamādi.

**Majjhelopo** tāva: Kattukāmo (Vin. ii, 82), kumbhakāraputto (Vin. i, 50), Vedallaṃ icc evamādi.

**Uttaralopo** tāva: Bhikkhu, bhikkhunī icc evamādi.

**Ādi-āgamo** tāva: Vutto Bhagavatā (Khu. i, 195) icc evamādi.

**Majjheāgamo** tāva: Sa sīlavā, sa paññavā icc evamādi.

**Uttara-āgamo** tāva: Vedallaṃ (Vin. i, 50) icc evamādi.

**Ādivikāro** tāva: Ārisyaṃ, āṇyaṃ, āsabhaṃ, ajjavaṃ icc evamādi.

**Majjhevikāro** tāva: Varārisyaṃ, parārisyaṃ[^47] icc evamādi.

**Uttaravikāro** tāva: Yāni, tāni, sukhāni icc evamādi.

**Ādiviparīto** tāva: Uggate sūriye uggacchati icc evamādi.

**Majjheviparīto** tāva: Samuggacchati, samuggate sūriye icc evamādi.

**Uttaraviparīto** tāva: Digu, diguṇaṃ[^48] (Vin. iii, 402) icc evamādi.

**Ādi-ādeso** tāva: Yūnaṃ icc evamādi.

**Majjhe-ādeso** tāva: Nyāyogā icc evamādi.

**Uttara-ādeso** tāva: sabbaseyyo, sabbaseṭṭho, cittaṃ icc evamādi. Evaṃ yathājinavacanānuparodhena sabbattha yojetabbā.

---

And in those [places there are] strengthening, elision, insertion, change, reversal and substitution.

In those [three places] –initial, middle and final–, without contradicting the Word of the Victorious One, sometimes there is strengthening (vuddhi), sometimes elision (lopa), sometimes insertion (āgama), sometimes change (vikāra), sometimes reversal (viparīta) and sometimes substitution (ādesa).

First, **initial strengthening**: **Ābhidhammiko**, **Venateyyo**, etc.

**Middle strengthening**: **sukhaseyyaṃ** (sleeping in comfort), **sukhakāri** dānaṃ (generosity that causes happiness), **sukhakāri** sīlaṃ (virtue that causes happiness), etc.

**Final strengthening**: **Kāliṅgo** (a resident of Kaliṅga), **Māgadhiko** (a resident of Magadha), **paccakkhadhammā** (one who has realized the Dhamma), etc.

**Initial elision**: **tālīsaṃ** (forty), etc.

**Middle elision**: **kattukāmo** (one who wishes to do), **kumbhakāraputto** (the son of the potter), **Vedallaṃ**, etc.

**Final elision**: **bhikkhu**, **bhikkhunī**, etc.

**Initial insertion**: **vutto** Bhagavatā (said by the Blessed One), etc.

**Middle insertion**: **sa** sīlavā (he is virtuous), **sa** paññavā (he is wise), etc.

**Final insertion**: **Vedallaṃ**, etc.

**Initial change**: **ārisyaṃ** (sageness), **āṇyaṃ** (indebtedness), **āsabhaṃ** (bullness), **ajjavaṃ** (rectitude), etc.

**Middle change**: **varārisyaṃ** (excellent state of a sage), **parārisyaṃ** (another state of a sage), etc.

**Final change**: **yāni** (those), **tāni** (those), **sukhāni** (happinesses), etc.

**Initial reversal**: uggate sūriye **uggacchati** (when the sun has risen: it rises), etc.

**Middle reversal**: **samuggacchati**, samuggate sūriye, etc.

**Final reversal**: **digu**, **diguṇaṃ** (two layers), etc.

**Initial substitution**: **yūnaṃ** (of ‘i’ and ‘u’), etc.

**Middle substitution**: **nyāyogā** (constant effort), etc.

**Final substitution**: **sabbaseyyo** (the noblest of all), **sabbaseṭṭho** (the best of all), **cittaṃ** (diversified/consciousness), etc. Thus they are to be formed everywhere without contradicting the Word of the Victorious One.

**405\. 365\. A-y-uvaṇṇānañ c’ ā-y-o vuddhi (860).** \[A-yu-vaṇṇānaṃ \+ ca \+ āyo \+ vuddhi, 4\]

**A** iti akāro, **i ī** iti **i**vaṇṇo, **u ū** iti **u**vaṇṇo, tesaṃ **a**kāra-**i**vaṇṇ’-**u**vaṇṇānaṃ **ā-e-o**vuddhiyo honti yathāsaṅkhyaṃ, **ā-ī-ū**vuddhi ca.[^49]

Ābhidhammiko, Venateyyo, oḷumpiko.

Pana **vuddhi**ggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ? Uttarapadavuddhibhāvatthaṃ, Aṅgamagadhehi āgatā ti Aṅgamāgadhikā (Vin. iii, 37). Nigamajanapadesu jātā ti negamajānapadā. Purimajanapadesu jātā ti porimajānapadā.[^50] Sattāhe niyutto ti sattāhikā.[^51] Catuvijje niyutto ti cātuvijjikā[^52] icc evamādī yojetabbā.

**Vuddhi** icc anena kvattho? Vuddhādisarassa vā ’saṃyogantassa saṇe ca.

---

‘Ā’, ‘e’ and ‘o’ are the strengthening of ‘a’, of ‘i’, ‘ī’ and of ‘u’, ‘ū’.

‘A’ is the letter ‘a’ (akāra); ‘i’ and ‘ī’ are [the pair of letters] ‘i’ (ivaṇṇa); ‘u’ and ‘ū’ are [the pair of letters] ‘u’ (uvaṇṇa); ‘ā’, ‘e’ and ‘o’ are respectively the strengthening (vuddhi) of the letter ‘a’, of [the pair of letters] ‘i’ and of [the pair of letters] ‘u’; and ‘ā’, ‘ī’ and ‘ū’ are also strengthening.

---

Examples:

1. **Ābhidhammiko**, **Venateyyo**, **oḷumpiko**.

And what is the purpose of taking “vuddhi” [again]? So that there is strengthening of the final member of the word.

Examples:

1. Aṅgamagadhehi āgatā ti **Aṅgamāgadhikā** (come from Aṅga and Magadha).
2. Nigamajanapadesu jātā ti **negamajānapadā** (born in the districts of the market town).
3. Purimajanapadesu jātā ti **porimajānapadā** (born in the eastern districts).
4. Sattāhe niyutto ti **sattāhikā** (occupied for seven days).
5. Catuvijje niyutto ti **cātuvijjikā** (dedicated to the four sciences).

And so the remaining [cases] are to be formed.

What is the use of the name “vuddhi”? [To employ it in suttas like] “Vuddhādisarassa vā ’saṃyogantassa saṇe ca” (§400).
