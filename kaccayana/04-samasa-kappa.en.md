**4-SAMĀSA-KAPPA**
**4-COMPOUND CHAPTER**

**SATTAMA-KAṆḌA**
**SEVENTH SECTION**

[This chapter deals with the different types of compounds.]

**316. 331. Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho (675).** [Nāmānaṃ + samāso + yuttattho, 3]

Tesaṃ nāmānaṃ payujjamānapadatthānaṃ yo yuttattho, so samāsasañño hoti.

Kathinadussaṃ (Vin. iii, 352); āgantukabhattaṃ (Vin. iii, 460); jīvitindriyaṃ (Abhi. i, 20, 168); samaṇabrāhmaṇā (D. i, 6; M. i, 114-5); Sāriputtamoggallānā (Vin. iii, 50; M. ii, 119); brāhmaṇagahapatikā (A. i, 386; ii, 26).

**Nāmānam** iti kimatthaṃ? Devadatto pacati. Yaññadatto pacati.

**Yuttattho** ti kimatthaṃ? Bhaṭo rañño; putto Devadattassa.

**Samāsa** icc anena kvattho? Kvaci samāsantagatānam akāranto.

---

The connected meaning of nouns [has the name] “samāsa” (compound).

The connected meaning[^1] of those nouns that have connected words and meanings: that has the name “samāsa” (compound).

---

Examples:

1. **Kathinadussaṃ** (a cloth for Kathina).
2. **Āgantukabhattaṃ** (a meal for the guest).
3. **Jīvitindriyaṃ** (life faculty).
4. **Samaṇabrāhmaṇā** (monks and brahmins).
5. **Sāriputtamoggallānā** (Sāriputta and Moggallāna).
6. **Brāhmaṇagahapatikā** (brahmins and householders).

What is the purpose of saying “nāmānaṃ” (of nouns)? To prevent the formation of compounds with those which are not nouns, such as in the following examples: Devadatto pacati (Devadatta cooks). Yaññadatto pacati (Yaññadatta cooks).

What is the purpose of saying “yuttattho” (connected meaning)? To prevent the formation of compounds when the words are not connected in meaning, such as in the following examples: Bhaṭo rañño (soldier of the king); putto Devadattassa (son of Devadatta).

What is the use of the name “samāsa” (compound)? For the use of the name “samāsa” in the sutta “Kvaci samāsantagatānam akāranto” (§337). Sometimes there is ‘a’ of the end vowel [of the nouns] that stand at the end of a compound.

**317. 332. Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca (676-7).** [Tesaṃ + vibhattiyo + lopā + ca, 4]

Tesaṃ yuttatthānaṃ samāsānaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca honti.

Kathinadussaṃ (Vin. iii, 352); āgantukabhattaṃ (Vin. iii, 460).

**Tesaṃ**gahaṇena samāsa-taddhit’-ākhyāta-kitakānaṃ vibhatti-paccaya-pad’-akkhar’-āgamā ca lopā honti.[^2] Vāsiṭṭho (D. iii, 66; M. ii, 407); venateyyo (Khu. v, 204).

**Ca**ggahaṇam avadhāraṇatthaṃ. Pabhaṅkaro (Khu. i, 430); amantandado (S. i, 29); Medhaṅkaro (Khu. iv, 381); Dīpaṅkaro (Khu. iv, 323-381).

---

And the inflections of those are elided.

And the inflections of those compounds that have connected meaning are elided.

---

Examples:

1. **Kathinadussaṃ** (a cloth for the Kathina).
2. **Āgantukabhattaṃ** (a meal for the guest).

By taking “tesaṃ”, the inflections (vibhatti), suffixes (paccaya), words (pada), syllables (akkhara) and insertions (āgama) of compounds (samāsa), secondary derivatives (taddhita), verbs (ākhyāta) and primary derivatives (kitaka) are also elided.

Examples:

1. **Vāsiṭṭho**.
2. **Venateyyo**.

Taking “ca” is for the purpose of restricting [the scope of this sutta].

Examples:

1. **Pabhaṅkaro** (maker of light (the sun)).
2. **Amantandado** (giver of the Deathless).
3. **Medhaṅkaro**.
4. **Dīpaṅkaro**.

**319. 330. Upasagga-nipātapubbako abyayībhāvo (696).** [Upasagga-nipātapubbako + abyayībhāvo, 2]

**Upasagga-nipāta**pubbako samāso **abyayībhāva**sañño hoti.

Nagarassa samīpe pavattati kathā iti upanagaraṃ; darathānaṃ abhāvo niddarathaṃ; makasānaṃ[^3] abhāvo nimmakasaṃ; vuḍḍhānaṃ paṭipāṭi yathāvuḍḍhaṃ (VinA. i, 10); ye ye vuḍḍhā vā yathāvuḍḍhaṃ (VinA. i, 10); jīvassa yattako paricchedo yāvajīvaṃ (Vin. i, 27; iii, 63); cittam adhikicca pavattanti te dhammā ti adhicittaṃ (Vin. iii, 278; A. i, 232); pabbatassa tiro tiropabbataṃ (D. i, 73; M. i, 41; A. i, 170); sotassa pati pavattati nāvā iti patisotaṃ; pāsādassa anto antopāsādaṃ.

**Abyayībhāvam** icc anena kvattho? Aṃvibhattīnam akārantā abyayībhāvā.

---

That which has a prefix or a particle in front is an “abyayībhāva” (adverbial compound).[^4]

The compound that has a prefix or a particle in front has the name “abyayībhāva” (adverbial compound).

---

Examples:

1. Nagarassa samīpe pavattati kathā iti **upanagaraṃ** (the talk that arises near the city).
2. Darathānaṃ abhāvo **niddarathaṃ** (absence of distress).
3. Makasānaṃ abhāvo **nimmakasaṃ** (absence of mosquitoes).
4. Vuḍḍhānaṃ paṭipāṭi **yathāvuḍḍhaṃ** (following the order of the older ones, according to seniority).
5. Ye ye vuḍḍhā vā **yathāvuḍḍhaṃ** (or whoever are the older ones, [according to them]).
6. Jīvassa yattako paricchedo **yāvajīvaṃ** (as far as life goes).
7. Cittam adhikicca pavattanti te dhammā ti **adhicittaṃ** (those states that exist referring to the mind).
8. Pabbatassa tiro **tiropabbataṃ** (across the mountain).
9. Sotassa pati pavattati nāvā iti **patisotaṃ** (the ship that goes against the current).
10. Pāsādassa anto **antopāsādaṃ** (inside the mansion).

What is the use of the name “abyayībhāva” (adverbial compound)? For the use of the name “abyayībhāva” in the sutta “Aṃvibhattīnam akārantā abyayībhāvā” (§341). After an adverbial compound that ends in ‘a’, the inflections become ‘aṃ’.

**320. 335. So napuṃsakaliṅgo (698).** [So + napuṃsakaliṅgo, 2]

So abyayībhāvasamāso napuṃsakaliṅgo ’va daṭṭhabbo.

Kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā iti adhikumāri; vadhuyā samīpe pavattati kathā iti upavadhu; gaṅgāya samīpe pavattati kathā iti upagaṅgaṃ; maṇikāya samīpe pavattati kathā iti upamaṇikaṃ.

---

It has the neuter gender.

That adverbial compound should be regarded as [belonging] to the neuter gender.

---

Examples:

1. Kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā iti **adhikumāri** (the talk that arises referring to maidens).
2. Vadhuyā samīpe pavattati kathā iti **upavadhu** (the talk that arises near the daughter-in-law).
3. Gaṅgāya samīpe pavattati kathā iti **upagaṅgaṃ** (the talk that arises near the Ganges).
4. Maṇikāya samīpe pavattati kathā iti **upamaṇikaṃ** (the talk that arises near the big vessel).

**321. 349. Diguss’ ekattaṃ (699).** [Digussa + ekattaṃ, 2]

Digussa samāsassa ekattaṃ hoti, napuṃsakaliṅgattañ ca.

Tayo lokā tilokaṃ; tayo daṇḍā tidaṇḍaṃ; tīṇi nayanāni tinayanaṃ; tayo siṅgā tisiṅgaṃ. Catasso disā catuddisaṃ (D. i, 243); pañca indriyāni pañcindriyaṃ (A. i, 468).

---

There is the state of one of a “digu” (numerical compound).

There is the state of one (singular) of a “digu” compound (numerical compound) and the state of the neuter gender.

---

Examples:

1. Tayo lokā **tilokaṃ** (the three worlds).
2. Tayo daṇḍā **tidaṇḍaṃ** (three sticks).
3. Tīṇi nayanāni **tinayanaṃ** (three eyes).
4. Tayo siṅgā **tisiṅgaṃ** (three horns).
5. Catasso disā **catuddisaṃ** (four directions).
6. Pañca indriyāni **pañcindriyaṃ** (five faculties).

**322. 359. Tathā dvande pāṇitūriyayoggasenaṅga-khuddajantuka-vividhaviruddha-visabhāgatthādīnañ ca (700).** [Tathā + dvande + pāṇitūriyayoggasenaṅga-khuddajantuka-vividhaviruddha-visabhāgattha-ādīnaṃ + ca, 4]

Tathā dvande samāse **pāṇitūriyayoggasenaṅga-khuddajantuka-vividhaviruddha-visabhāgattha** icc evamādīnaṃ ekattaṃ hoti, napuṃsakaliṅgattañ ca.

Taṃ yathā? Cakkhu ca sotañ ca cakkhusotaṃ (Khu. i, 211); mukhañ ca nāsikā ca mukhanāsikaṃ; chavi ca maṃsañ ca lohitañ ca chavimaṃsalohitaṃ. Evaṃ **pāṇyaṅgatthe**.

Saṅkho ca paṇavo ca saṅkhapaṇavaṃ (D. i, 75); gītañ ca vāditañ ca gītavāditaṃ (D. i, 5); daddari ca ḍiṇḍimo ca daddariḍiṇḍimaṃ.[^5] Evaṃ **tūriyaṅgatthe**.

Phālo ca pācanañ ca phālapācanaṃ (S. i, 175; Khu. i, 291; SuttanipataA. 132);[^6] yugañ ca naṅgalañ ca yuganaṅgalaṃ (S. i, 175; Khu. i, 291; SuttanipataA. 132).[^7] Evaṃ **yoggaṅgatthe**.

Asi ca cammañ ca asicammaṃ (M. i, 121; A. ii, 82); dhanu ca kalāpo ca dhanukalāpaṃ (M. i, 121; A. ii, 82); hatthī ca asso ca hatthi-assaṃ; ratho ca pattiko ca rathapattikaṃ. Evaṃ **senaṅgatthe**.

Ḍaṃsā ca makasā ca ḍaṃsamakasaṃ, kuntho ca kipilliko ca kunthakipillikaṃ, kīṭo ca sarīsapo ca kīṭasarīsapaṃ. Evaṃ **khuddajantukatthe**.

Ahi ca nakulo ca ahinakulaṃ (DhA. i, 32); biḷāro ca mūsiko ca biḷāramūsikaṃ; kāko ca ulūko ca kākolūkaṃ (DhA. i, 32). Evaṃ **vividhaviruddhatthe**.

Sīlañ ca paññāṇañ ca sīlapaññāṇaṃ (A. ii, 26); samatho ca vipassanā ca samathavipassanaṃ (AbhA. i, 240); vijjā ca caraṇañ ca vijjācaraṇaṃ (A. ii, 2). Evaṃ **visabhāgatthe**.

**Ādiggahaṇaṃ** kimatthaṃ? Dāsī ca dāso ca dāsidāsaṃ (D. i, 5; M. ii, 366; Khu. vii, 37); itthī ca pumā ca itthipumaṃ; patto ca cīvarañ ca pattacīvaraṃ (Vin. i, 11; iii, 130); chattañ ca upāhanā ca chattupāhanaṃ (Vin. ii, 451; DhA. i, 241); tikañ ca catukkañ ca tikacatukkaṃ; veno ca rathakāro ca venarathakāraṃ; sākuṇiko ca māgaviko ca sākuṇikamāgavikaṃ; dīgho ca majjhimo ca dīghamajjhimaṃ icc evamādi.

---

Likewise, in a copulative compound (dvanda) there is also [singular and neuter gender] of parts of beings (pāṇi-aṅga), musical instruments (tūriya-aṅga), parts of a vehicle (yogga-aṅga), parts of an army (senaṅga), small creatures (khuddajantuka), different enemies (vividhaviruddha), opposite meaning (visabhāgattha), etc. (ādi).

Likewise, in a copulative compound there is singular and neuter gender of parts of beings, musical instruments, parts of a vehicle, parts of an army, small creatures, different enemies and opposite meaning, etc.

---

As what?

Examples:

1. Cakkhu ca sotañ ca **cakkhusotaṃ** (eye and ear).
2. Mukhañ ca nāsikā ca **mukhanāsikaṃ** (mouth and nose).
3. Chavi ca maṃsañ ca lohitañ ca **chavimaṃsalohitaṃ** (skin, flesh and blood).

Thus in the meaning of parts of beings.

1. Saṅkho ca paṇavo ca **saṅkhapaṇavaṃ** (conch and small drum).
2. Gītañ ca vāditañ ca **gītavāditaṃ** (singing and playing).
3. Daddari ca ḍiṇḍimo ca **daddariḍiṇḍimaṃ** (drums).

Thus in the meaning of musical instruments.

1. Phālo ca pācanañ ca **phālapācanaṃ** (ploughshare and goad).
2. Yugañ ca naṅgalañ ca **yuganaṅgalaṃ** (yoke and plough).

Thus in the meaning of parts of a vehicle.

1. Asi ca cammañ ca **asicammaṃ** (sword and shield).
2. Dhanu ca kalāpo ca **dhanukalāpaṃ** (bow and quiver).
3. Hatthī ca asso ca **hatthi-assaṃ** (elephant and horse).
4. Ratho ca pattiko ca **rathapattikaṃ** (chariot and soldier).

Thus in the meaning of parts of an army.

1. Daṃsā ca makasā ca **daṃsamakasaṃ** (fly and mosquito).
2. Kuntho ca kipilliko ca **kunthakipillikaṃ** (ant and white ant).
3. Kīṭo ca sarīsapo ca **kīṭasarīsapaṃ** (moth and snake).

Thus in the meaning of small creatures.

1. Ahi ca nakulo ca **ahinakulaṃ** (snake and mongoose).
2. Biḷāro ca mūsiko ca **biḷāramūsikaṃ** (cat and mouse).
3. Kāko ca ulūko ca **kākolūkaṃ** (crow and owl).

Thus in the meaning of different enemies.

1. Sīlañ ca paññāṇañ ca **sīlapaññāṇaṃ** (virtue and wisdom).
2. Samatho ca vipassanā ca **samathavipassanaṃ** (tranquility and insight).
3. Vijjā ca caraṇañ ca **vijjācaraṇaṃ** (vision and conduct).

Thus in the meaning of opposites.

What is the purpose of taking “ādi” (etcetera)? To include the following: Dāsī ca dāso ca **dāsidāsaṃ** (female and male slave); itthī ca pumā ca **itthipumaṃ** (female and male); patto ca cīvarañ ca **pattacīvaraṃ** (bowl and robe); chattañ ca upāhanā ca **chattupāhanaṃ** (umbrella and sandals); tikañ ca catukkañ ca **tikacatukkaṃ** (triad and quartet); veno ca rathakāro ca **venarathakāraṃ** (bamboo weaver and chariot maker); sākuṇiko ca māgaviko ca **sākuṇikamāgavikaṃ** (fowler and hunter); dīgho ca majjhimo ca **dīghamajjhimaṃ** (long and middle), etc.

**323. 360. Vibhāsā rukkha-tiṇa-pasu-dhana-dhañña-janapadādīnañ ca (701).** [Vibhāsā + rukkha-tiṇa-pasu-dhana-dhañña-janapada-ādīnaṃ + ca, 3]

**Rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapada** icc evamādīnaṃ vibhāsā ekattaṃ hoti, napuṃsakaliṅgattañ ca dvande samāse.

Assattho ca kapītano[^8] ca assatthakapītanaṃ, assatthakapītanā vā. Usīrañ ca bīraṇañ ca usīrabīraṇaṃ, usīrabīraṇā vā. Ajo ca eḷako ca ajeḷakaṃ, ajeḷakā (D. i, 5; M. i, 217; A. i, 351) vā. Hiraññañ ca suvaṇṇañ ca hiraññasuvaṇṇaṃ (D. i, 108; M. ii, 258), hiraññasuvaṇṇā vā. Sāli ca yavo ca sāliyavaṃ, sāliyavā vā. Kāsī ca Kosalā ca Kāsikosalaṃ, Kāsikosalā vā.

**Ādi**ggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ? Sāvajjañ ca anavajjañ ca sāvajjānavajjaṃ (A. i, 126), sāvajjānavajjā vā. Hīnañ ca paṇītañ ca hīnapaṇītaṃ (A. i, 126), hīnapaṇītā vā. Kaṇho ca sukko ca kaṇhasukkaṃ (A. i, 553), kaṇhasukkā vā.

---

And facultatively of “rukkha” (tree), “tiṇa” (grass), “pasu” (beast), “dhana” (wealth), “dhañña” (grain), “janapada” (district), etc.

In a copulative compound, [the words] “rukkha” (tree), “tiṇa” (grass), “pasu” (beast), “dhana” (wealth), “dhañña” (grain), “janapada” (district), etc., facultatively are singular and neuter.

---

Examples:

1. Assattho ca kapītano ca **assatthakapītanaṃ**, **assatthakapītanā** vā (banyan tree and wood-apple).
2. Usīrañ ca bīraṇañ ca **usīrabīraṇaṃ**, **usīrabīraṇā** vā (fragrant root and grass).
3. Ajo ca eḷako ca **ajeḷakaṃ**, **ajeḷakā** vā (goat and wild goat).
4. Hiraññañ ca suvaṇṇañ ca **hiraññasuvaṇṇaṃ**, **hiraññasuvaṇṇā** vā (silver and gold).
5. Sāli ca yavo ca **sāliyavaṃ**, **sāliyavā** vā (rice and barley).
6. Kāsī ca Kosalā ca **Kāsikosalaṃ**, **Kāsikosalā** vā (Kāsī and Kosalā).

What is the purpose of taking “ādi”? To allow the application of this rule in other cases: Sāvajjañ ca anavajjañ ca **sāvajjānavajjaṃ**, **sāvajjānavajjā** vā (faulty and faultless); hīnañ ca paṇītañ ca **hīnapaṇītaṃ**, **hīnapaṇītā** vā (low and excellent); kaṇho ca sukko ca **kaṇhasukkaṃ**, **kaṇhasukkā** vā (black and white).

**324. 339. Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo (702).** [Dvipade + tulyādhikaraṇe + kammadhārayo, 3]

Dve padāni tulyādhikaraṇāni yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso **kammadhāraya**sañño hoti.

Mahanto ca so puriso cā ti mahāpuriso (A. i, 346; Khu. i, 64); kaṇho ca so sappo cā ti kaṇhasappo (Vin. i, 24); nīlañ ca taṃ uppalañ cā ti nīluppalaṃ; lohitañ ca taṃ candanañ cā ti lohitacandanaṃ; brāhmaṇī ca sā dārikā cā ti brāhmaṇadārikā; khattiyā ca sā kaññā cā ti khattiyakaññā (M. i, 122; A. i, 526).

**Kammadhāraya** icc anena kvattho? Kammadhārayasaññe ca.

---

When two words referring to the same thing [are compounded], there is a “kammadhāraya” (adjectival compound).

When two words referring to the same thing (tulyādhikaraṇa) are compounded, then that compound has the name “kammadhāraya” (adjectival compound).

---

Examples:

1. Mahanto ca so puriso cā ti **mahāpuriso** (great man).
2. Kaṇho ca so sappo cā ti **kaṇhasappo** (black snake (cobra)).
3. Nīlañ ca taṃ uppalañ cā ti **nīluppalaṃ** (blue lily).
4. Lohitañ ca taṃ candanañ cā ti **lohitacandanaṃ** (red sandal).
5. Brāhmaṇī ca sā dārikā cā ti **brāhmaṇadārikā** (brahmin girl).
6. Khattiyā ca sā kaññā cā ti **khattiyakaññā** (noble girl).

What is the use of the name “kammadhāraya” (adjectival compound)? For the use of the name “kammadhāraya” in the sutta “Kammadhārayasaññe ca” (§332). Also in an adjectival compound.

**325. 348. Saṅkhyāpubbo digu (703).** [Saṅkhyāpubbo + digu, 2]

The adjectival compound that has a numeral as its first member has the name “digu” (numerical compound).

---

What is the use of the name “digu” (numerical compound)? For the use of the name “digu” in the sutta “Diguss’ ekattaṃ” (§321). There is the state of one of a numerical compound.

**326. 341. Ubhe tappurisā (707).** [Ubhe + tappurisā, 2]

Ubhe digukammadhārayasamāsā **tappurisa**saññā honti.

**Tappurisa** icc anena kvattho? Attaṃ n’ assa tappurise.

---

Both are “tappurisa” (determinative compounds).

Both, the numerical compound and the adjectival compound, have the name “tappurisa” (determinative compound).

---

Examples:

1. Na brāhmaṇo **abrāhmaṇo** (not a brahmin).

5. Na pañcapūlī **apañcapūlī** (not five bundles).
6. Na sattagodāvaraṃ **asattagodāvaraṃ** (not seven Godāvarī rivers).
7. Na dasagavaṃ **adasagavaṃ** (not ten cows).
8. Na pañcagavaṃ **apañcagavaṃ** (not five cows).

What is the use of the name “tappurisa” (determinative compound)? For the use of the name “tappurisa” in the sutta “Attaṃ n’ assa tappurise” (§333). In a determinative compound, there is ‘a’ of “na”.

**327. 351. Amādayo parapadebhi (704).** [Aṃ-ādayo + parapadebhi, 2]

Tā **am**ādayo nāmehi parapadebhi yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso **tappurisa**sañño hoti.

Bhūmiṃ gato bhūmigato (M. ii, 258; S. i, 102); sabbarattiṃ sobhaṇo sabbarattisobhaṇo; apāyaṃ gato apāyagato; issarena kataṃ issarakataṃ; sallena viddho sallaviddho (M. ii, 92; Khu. vii, 4); kathinassa dussaṃ kathinadussaṃ (Vin. iii, 352); āgantukassa bhattaṃ āgantukabhattaṃ (Vin. iii, 406); methunā apeto methunāpeto; corā bhayaṃ corabhayaṃ; rañño putto rājaputto (D. i, 49; Khu. iii, 205); Dhaññānaṃ rāsi dhaññarāsi; rūpe saññā rūpasaññā (A. iii, 211); saṃsāre dukkhaṃ saṃsāradukkhaṃ.

---

‘Aṃ’, etc., with the following nouns.

When the [inflections] ‘aṃ’, etc., are compounded with the nouns [that are] following members, then that compound has the name “tappurisa” (determinative compound).

---

Examples:

1. Bhūmiṃ gato **bhūmigato** (gone to the earth).
2. Sabbarattiṃ sobhaṇo **sabbarattisobhaṇo** (beautiful for the whole night).
3. Apāyaṃ gato **apāyagato** (gone to hell).
4. Issarena kataṃ **issarakataṃ** (made by the creator).
5. Sallena viddho **sallaviddho** (pierced by an arrow).
6. Kathinassa dussaṃ **kathinadussaṃ** (a cloth for Kathina).
7. Āgantukassa bhattaṃ **āgantukabhattaṃ** (a meal for the guest).
8. Methunā apeto **methunāpeto** (one who refrains from sexual intercourse).
9. Corā bhayaṃ **corabhayaṃ** (fear or danger from thieves).
10. Rañño putto **rājaputto** (son of the king).
11. Dhaññānaṃ rāsi **dhaññarāsi** (a heap of grain).
12. Rūpe saññā **rūpasaññā** (perception in form).
13. Saṃsāre dukkhaṃ **saṃsāradukkhaṃ** (the suffering in the round of rebirths).

**Bahubbīhi** icc anena kvattho? Bahubbīhimhi ca.

---

In the meaning of other words there is a “bahubbīhi” (relative compound).

When two nouns or many nouns are compounded in the meaning of other words,[^11] then that compound has the name “bahubbīhi” (relative compound).

---

Examples:

1. Āgatā samaṇā imaṃ saṃghārāmaṃ so ’yaṃ **āgatasamaṇo**, saṃghārāmo (the monks came to this monastery of the Saṃgha; that is “āgatasamaṇo” [where the monks came], the monastery of the Saṃgha [is what is meant]).
2. Jitāni indriyāni anena samaṇena so ’yaṃ **jitindriyo**, samaṇo (the faculties are subdued by this monk; he is “jitindriyo” [one whose faculties are subdued], the monk [is what is meant]).
3. Dinno suṅkho yassa rañño so ’yaṃ **dinnasuṅkho**, rājā (tax is given to that king; he is “dinnasuṅkho” [to whom tax is given], the king [is what is meant]).
4. Niggatā janā asmā gāmā so ’yaṃ **niggatajano**, gāmo (people went out from this village; that is “niggatajano” [from which people went out], the village [is what is meant]).
5. Chinno hattho yassa purisassa so ’yaṃ **chinnahattho**, puriso (the hand of that man is cut off; he is “chinnahattho” [one whose hand has been cut off], the man [is what is meant]).
6. Sampannāni sassāni yasmiṃ janapade so ’yaṃ **sampannasasso**, janapado (grains are abundant in that district; that is “sampannasasso” [a place where grains are abundant], the district [is what is meant]).

**Nigrodhassa parimaṇḍalo nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo**:

The circumference (parimaṇḍalo) of the banyan tree (nigrodhassa) [is called] banyan tree circumference (nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo). That prince (yo rājakumāro) whose circumference (parimaṇḍalo) is like (iva) the banyan tree circumference (nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo), he is “nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo” [one whose circumference is like that of the banyan tree].

Alternatively (atha vā): the proportions (parimaṇḍalo) of that prince (yassa rājakumārassa) are like (iva) the banyan tree circumference (nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo); he is “nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo”, the prince [is what is meant].

**Cakkhuno bhūto cakkhubhūto**:

Being (bhūto) of the eye (cakkhuno) [is called] cakkhubhūto. This Blessed One (yo Bhagavā) is a being (bhūto) who is like (iva) the eye (cakkhubhūto); he is “cakkhubhūto” [one who is like the eye], the Blessed One [is what is meant].

**Suvaṇṇassa vaṇṇo suvaṇṇavaṇṇo**:

The color (vaṇṇo) of gold (suvaṇṇassa) [is called] golden color (suvaṇṇavaṇṇo). The complexion (vaṇṇo) of this Blessed One (yassa Bhagavato) is like (viya) golden color (suvaṇṇavaṇṇo); he is “suvaṇṇavaṇṇo” [one whose complexion is like golden color], the Blessed One [is what is meant].

**Brahmuno saro brahmassaro**:

The voice (saro) of Brahmā (brahmuno) [is called] voice of Brahmā (brahmassaro). The voice (saro) of this Blessed One (yassa Bhagavato) is like (viya) the voice of Brahmā (brahmassaro); he is “brahmassaro” [one whose voice is like that of Brahmā], the Blessed One [is what is meant].

**Sayaṃ-patita-paṇṇa-puppha-phala-vāyu-toy’-āhārā**:

Leaf and (paṇṇañ ca) flower and (pupphañ ca) fruit (phalañ ca) [are called] leaves, flowers and fruits (paṇṇapupphaphalāni). [dvanda-samāsa]

Fallen down (patitāni) by themselves (sayam eva), fallen down by themselves (sayaṃpatitāni). [kammadhāraya-samāsa]

Fallen down by themselves (sayaṃpatitāni) and (ca) those (tāni) [are] leaves, flowers and fruits (paṇṇapupphaphalāni ca), so (iti) [they are called] leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalāni). [kammadhāraya-samāsa]

Air and (vāyu ca) water (toyañ ca) [are called] air and water (vāyutoyāni). [dvanda-samāsa]

Leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves and (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalāni ca) air and water (vāyutoyāni ca), so (iti) [they are called] leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves and air and water (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalavāyutoyāni). [dvanda-samāsa]

Those whose nutriment is (āhārā yesaṃ) leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves and air and water (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalavāyutoyāni), they (te) [are called] those that have leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves and air and water as nutriment (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalavāyutoyāhārā). The sages (isayo) [are what is meant].

What is to be said here (yam ettha vattabbaṃ) has been said below (actually, above) (taṃ heṭṭhā vuttam eva).

This is (ayaṃ pana) a relative compound which has the same location (tulyādhikaraṇabahubbīhi) and which has a copulative compound and an adjectival compound in it (dvanda-kammadhāraya-gabbho).

Alternatively (atha vā): those whose nutriment is (āhārā yesaṃ) by means of leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves and air and water (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalavāyutoyehi), they (te) [are called] those that have leaves, flowers and fruits fallen down by themselves and air and water as nutriment (sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalavāyutoyāhārā). This is (ayaṃ pana) a relative compound which does not have the same location (bhinnādhikaraṇabahubbīhi).

**Nānā-duma-patita-puppha-vāsita-sānū**:

Different (nānā), [that is,] various (pakārā) trees (dumā) [are called] various trees (nānādumā). [kammadhāraya-samāsa]

Fallen down (patitāni) from various trees (nānādumehi) [is called] fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitāni). [tappurisa-samāsa]

Fallen down from various trees and (nānādumapatitāni ca) those flowers (tāni pupphāni ca), so (iti) [they are called] flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphāni). [kammadhāraya-samāsa]

Perfumed (vāsitā) by flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphehi) [is called] perfumed by flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphavāsitā). [tappurisa-samāsa]

The valleys (sānū) perfumed by flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphavāsitā) of that king of mountains (yassa pabbatarājassa), that is (so ’yaṃ) [one which has] valleys perfumed by flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphavāsitasānu), the king of mountains [is what is meant].

This is (ayaṃ pana) a relative compound which has the same location (tulyādhikaraṇabahubbīhi) and which has an adjectival compound and a determinative compound in it (kammadhāraya-tappurisagabbho).

Alternatively (atha vā): perfumed (vāsitā) valleys (sānū) [are called] perfumed valleys (vāsitasānū). [kammadhāraya-samāsa] Although it is looking to another place (sāpekkhatte sati pi), because it can convey the meaning (gamakattā), it is a compound (samāso). The valleys perfumed (vāsitasānū) by flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphehi) of that king of mountains (yassa pabbatarājassa), that is one which has valleys perfumed by flowers fallen down from various trees (nānādumapatitapupphavāsitasānu), the king of mountains [is what is meant].

This is a relative compound which does not have the same location (bhinnādhikaraṇabahubbīhi).

**Dvanda** icc anena kvattho? Dvandaṭṭhā vā.

---

The collection of nouns having the same inflection has the name “dvanda” (copulative compound).

---

Examples:

6. Kuvero ca Vāsavo ca **Kuveravāsavā** (Kuvera and Vāsava).

What is the use of the name “dvanda” (copulative compound)? For the use of the name “dvanda” in the sutta “Dvandaṭṭhā vā” (§165). Optionally, in a copulative compound.

**330. 340. Mahataṃ mahā tulyādhikaraṇe pade (710).** [Mahataṃ + mahā + tulyādhikaraṇe + pade, 4]

Mahanto ca so puriso cā ti mahāpuriso (D. iii, 118; A. i, 346; A. ii, 349; Khu. i, 64); mahantī ca sā devī cā ti mahādevī (JA. i, 483); mahantañ ca taṃ balañ cā ti mahābalaṃ (Khu. iii, 55); mahanto ca so nāgo cā ti mahānāgo (D. ii, 207); mahanto ca so yaso cā ti mahāyaso; mahantañ ca taṃ padumavanañ cā ti mahāpadumavanaṃ; mahantī ca sā nadī cā ti mahānadī (A. ii, 474; S. iii, 44); mahanto ca so maṇi cā ti mahāmaṇi; mahanto ca so gahapatiko cā ti mahāgahapatiko; mahantañ ca taṃ dhanañ cā ti mahādhanaṃ (DhA. i, 151); mahanto ca so puñño cā ti mahāpuñño (Vin. i, 55).[^12]

**Bahuvacanaggahaṇena** kvaci **mahanta**saddassa **mah**ādeso hoti. Mahantañ ca taṃ phalañ cā ti mahapphalaṃ (A. i, 161); mahabbalaṃ (Khu. iii, 55). Evaṃ mahaddhanaṃ (Khu. iii, 49); mahabbhayaṃ (Khu. i, 395).

---

When there is a word having the same locus, there is “mahā” of [the words] “mahanta”.

When a word has the same locus (tulyādhikaraṇa), there is the substitution by “mahā” of the words “mahanta”.

---

Examples:

1. Mahanto ca so puriso cā ti **mahāpuriso** (great man).
2. Mahantī ca sā devī cā ti **mahādevī** (great queen).
3. Mahantañ ca taṃ balañ cā ti **mahābalaṃ** (great strength).
4. Mahanto ca so nāgo cā ti **mahānāgo** (great snake).
5. Mahanto ca so yaso cā ti **mahāyaso** (great fame).
6. Mahantañ ca taṃ padumavanañ cā ti **mahāpadumavanaṃ** (great forest of lotuses).
7. Mahantī ca sā nadī cā ti **mahānadī** (great river).
8. Mahanto ca so maṇi cā ti **mahāmaṇi** (great gem).
9. Mahanto ca so gahapatiko cā ti **mahāgahapatiko** (great householder).
10. Mahantañ ca taṃ dhanañ cā ti **mahādhanaṃ** (great wealth).
11. Mahanto ca so puñño cā ti **mahāpuñño** (great merit).

Sometimes, by taking the plural,[^13] the word “mahanta” is substituted by “maha”.

Examples:

1. Mahantañ ca taṃ phalañ cā ti **mahapphalaṃ** (great fruit).
2. Mahantañ ca taṃ balañ cā ti **mahabbalaṃ** (great strength).

Likewise:

1. Mahantañ ca taṃ dhanañ cā ti **mahaddhanaṃ** (great wealth).
2. Mahantañ ca taṃ bhayañ cā ti **mahabbhayaṃ** (great fear or danger).

---

**331. 353. Itthiyaṃ bhāsitapum’-itthī pumā ’va ce (714-5).** [Itthiyaṃ + bhāsita-puma-itthī + pumā + iva + ce, 5]

Itthiyaṃ tulyādhikaraṇe pade ce bhāsitapumitthī pumā ’va daṭṭhabbā.

Dīghā jaṅghā yassa so ’yaṃ dīghajaṅgho; kalyāṇabhariyo; pahūtapañño.

**Bhāsitapum**eti kimatthaṃ? Brāhmaṇabandhu ca sā bhariyā cā ti brāhmaṇabandhubhariyā.[^14]

---

In the feminine, if a feminine indicated masculine, [it should be regarded] as masculine.

When there is a word that has the same locus in the feminine, if it is a feminine that indicated masculine [in the past], it should be regarded as masculine.

---

Examples:

1. Dīghā jaṅghā yassa so ’yaṃ **dīghajaṅgho** (the legs of that [man] are long; he is “dīghajaṅgho” [one who has long legs]).
2. Kalyāṇā bhariyā yassa so ’yaṃ **kalyāṇabhariyo** ([one who has] a good wife).
3. Pahūtā paññā yassa so ’yaṃ **pahūtapañño** ([one who has] much wisdom).

What is the purpose of saying “bhāsitapuma” (that indicated masculine)? To prevent the operation of this rule when it did not indicate masculine in the past: Brāhmaṇabandhu ca sā bhariyā cā ti brāhmaṇabandhubhariyā (a relative of the brahmin and she is a wife, so [she is called] a wife that is a relative of a brahmin).[^15]

---

**332. 343. Kammadhārayasaññe ca (716).** [Kammadhārayasaññe + ca, 2]

Kammadhārayasaññe ca samāse itthiyaṃ tulyādhikaraṇe pade pubbe bhāsitapumitthī ce, pumā ’va daṭṭhabbā.

Brāhmaṇadārikā; khattiyakaññā (M. i, 122; A. i, 526); khattiyakumārikā (JA. i, 489).

**Bhāsitapum**eti kimatthaṃ? Khattiyabandhudārikā; brāhmaṇabandhudārikā.

---

Also in an adjectival compound.

Also when there is a word that has the same locus in the feminine in an adjectival compound, if the previous [word] is a feminine that indicated masculine [in the past], it should be regarded as masculine.

---

Examples:

1. **Brāhmaṇadārikā** (brahmin girl).
2. **Khattiyakaññā** (noble girl).
3. **Khattiyakumārikā** (noble maiden).

What is the purpose of saying “bhāsitapuma” (that indicated masculine)? To prevent the operation of this rule when it did not indicate masculine in the past, such as “khattiyabandhudārikā” (a girl that is a relative of a noble); “brāhmaṇabandhudārikā” (a girl that is a relative of a brahmin).[^16]

---

**333. 344. Attaṃ nassa tappurise (717).** [Attaṃ + nassa + tappurise, 3]

**Na**ssa padassa tappurise uttarapade **a**ttaṃ hoti.

Na brāhmaṇo abrāhmaṇo (D. iii, 67); avasalo; abhikkhu (Vin. ii, 412); apañcavassaṃ; apañcagavaṃ.

---

In a determinative compound, there is ‘a’ of “na”.

When there is a following word in a determinative compound, there is ‘a’ of “na”.

---

Examples:

1. Na brāhmaṇo **abrāhmaṇo** (not a brahmin).
2. Na vasalo **avasalo** (not an outcast).
3. Na bhikkhu **abhikkhu** (not a bhikkhu).
4. Na pañcavassaṃ **apañcavassaṃ** (not five rains).
5. Na pañcagavaṃ **apañcagavaṃ** (not five cows).

---

**334. 345. Sare an (718).** [Sare + an, 2]

**Na**ssa padassa tappurise **an**ādeso hoti sare pare.

Na asso anasso; anissaro (VinA. i, 339); anariyo (Vin. iii, 14).

---

When a vowel follows, there is ‘an’.

When a vowel follows in a determinative compound, the word “na” is substituted by ‘an’.

---

Examples:

1. Na asso **anasso** (not a horse, a mule).
2. Na issaro **anissaro** (not a creator).
3. Na ariyo **anariyo** (not a noble one).

---

**335. 346. Kad kussa (719).** [Kad + kussa, 2]

**Ku** icc etassa **kad** hoti sare pare.

Kucchitaṃ annaṃ kadannaṃ; kucchitaṃ asanaṃ kadasanaṃ.

**Sare** ti kimatthaṃ? Kucchitā dārā yesaṃ (apuññakārānaṃ) te hontī ti kudārā; kujanā. Evaṃ kuputtā, kugehā, kuvatthā, kudāsā.

---

There is ‘kad’ of ‘ku’.

When a vowel follows, ‘ku’ becomes ‘kad’.

---

Examples:

1. Kucchitaṃ annaṃ **kadannaṃ** (bad food).
2. Kucchitaṃ asanaṃ **kadasanaṃ** (bad aliment).

What is the purpose of saying “sare” (when a vowel follows)? [This sutta does not apply when a vowel does not follow, as in the following examples:]

Bad (kucchitā) wives (dārā) of those (yesaṃ) that do demerit (apuññakārānaṃ), they (te) are (honti) so (iti) those who have bad wives (kudārā); bad persons (kujanā). Likewise, those who have bad sons (kuputtā), those who have bad houses (kugehā), those who have bad clothes (kuvatthā), those who have bad female slaves (kudāsā).

---

**336. 347. Kā ’ppatthesu ca (720).** [Kā + appa-atthesu + ca, 3]

**Ku** icc etassa **kā** hoti appatthesu ca.

Kālavaṇaṃ; kāpupphaṃ.

**Bahuvacana**ggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ? **Ku** icc etassa anappakatthesu pi kvaci **kā** hoti. Kāpurisā (Khu. ii, 157; PetavatthuA. 117).

---

Also in the meanings of little, there is ‘kā’.

Also in the meanings of little, there is ‘kā’ of ‘ku’.

---

Examples:

1. **Kālavaṇaṃ** (little salt).
2. **Kāpupphaṃ** (little flower (few flowers)).

What is the purpose of taking “bahuvacana” (plural)? Sometimes, also in meanings other than little there is ‘kā’ of ‘ku’. Kāpurisā (bad men).

---

**337. 350. Kvaci samāsantagatānam akāranto (722).** [Kvaci + samāsa-antagatānaṃ + akāro + anto, 4]

**Samā**santagatānaṃ nāmānam anto saro kvaci **a**kāro hoti.

Devānaṃ rājā devarājo, devarājā (S. i, 236-7); devānaṃ sakhā devasakho, devasakhā; pañca ahāni pañcāhaṃ (Vin. iv, 112), sattāhaṃ (M. ii, 242; Khu. i, 87), pañcagavaṃ; chattupāhanaṃ (Vin. ii, 451); upasaradaṃ; visālakkho (Khu. vi, 241); vimukho.

**Kāra**ggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ? **Ā**kāranta-**i**kārantā[^17] ca honti. Paccakkhā dhammā yassa so ’yan ti paccakkhadhammā, surabhino gandho surabhigandhi; sundaro gandho sugandhi; pūtino gandho pūtigandhi; kucchito gandho kugandhi; duṭṭhu gandho yassa so ’yan ti duggandhi; pūti eva gandho pūtigandhi.

**Nadī**antā ca **kattu**antā ca **ka**paccayo hoti samāsante.

Bahū nadiyo yasmiṃ so ’yaṃ bahunadiko, janapado. Bahavo kattāro yassa so ’yaṃ bahukattuko, puriso.

---

Sometimes there is ‘a’ of the end vowel of those that stand at the end of a compound.

Sometimes, the end vowel of the nouns that stand at the end of a compound becomes ‘a’.

---

Examples:

1. Devānaṃ rājā **devarājo**, **devarājā** (king of the devas).
2. Devānaṃ sakhā **devasakho**, **devasakhā** (friend of the devas).
3. Pañca ahāni **pañcāhaṃ** (five days).
4. **Sattāhaṃ** (seven days).
5. **Pañcagavaṃ** (five cows).
6. **Chattupāhanaṃ** (umbrella and sandals).
7. **Upasaradaṃ** (near autumn).
8. **Visālakkho** ([one who has] large eyes).
9. **Vimukho** ([one who has] a deformed face).

What is the purpose of taking “kāra” [why does it say “akāranto” instead of “a-anto”]? To allow the substitutions by ‘ā’ and ‘i’.

Examples:

1. Paccakkhā dhammā yassa so ’yan ti **paccakkhadhammā** ([one who has] realized Dhammas).
2. Surabhino gandho **surabhigandhi** (scent of a fragrant flower).
3. Sundaro gandho **sugandhi** (good smell).
4. Pūtino gandho **pūtigandhi** (smell of something rotten).
5. Kucchito gandho **kugandhi** (bad smell).
6. Duṭṭhu gandho yassa so ’yan ti **duggandhi** ([one who has] bad smell).
7. Pūti eva gandho **pūtigandhi** (rotten smell).

At the end of the compound there is the suffix ‘ka’ of those that have “nadī” as the last member and of those that have “kattu” as the last member.

Examples:

1. Bahū nadiyo yasmiṃ so ’yaṃ **bahunadiko**, janapado (many rivers in that; that is “bahunadiko” [one which has many rivers], a district [is what is meant]).
2. Bahavo kattāro yassa so ’yaṃ **bahukattuko**, puriso (many helpers of that one; he is “bahukattuko” [one who has many helpers], a person [is what is meant]).

---

**338. 356. Nadimhā ca (725).** [Nadimhā + ca, 2]

**Nadi**mhā ca **ka**paccayo hoti samāsante.

Bahū nadiyo yasmiṃ so ’yan ti bahunadiko. Bahū kantiyo yassa so ’yan ti bahukantiko. Bahunāriko.

---

Also after “nadī”.[^18]

Also at the end of a compound, after “nadī”, there is the suffix ‘ka’.

---

Examples:

1. Bahū nadiyo yasmiṃ so ’yan ti **bahunadiko** (there are many rivers in this; this is [a place which has] many rivers).
2. Bahū kantiyo yassa so ’yan ti **bahukantiko** (there are many charms of this one; he is [one who has] many charms).
3. Bahū nārikā yassa so ’yan ti **bahunāriko** (there are many women of this one; he is [one who has] many women).

---

**339. 358. Jāyāya tudaṃ-jāni[^19]** **patimhi (731).** [Jāyāya + tudaṃ-jāni + patimhi, 3]

**Jāyā** icc etāya **tudaṃ jāni** icc ete ādesā honti **pati**mhi pare.

Tudaṃpatī,[^20] jānipatī.

---

When “pati” follows, there are “tudaṃ” and “jāni” of “jāyā”.

When “pati” follows, there are these substitutions of “jāyā”: “tudaṃ” and “jāni”.

---

Examples:

1. **Tudaṃpatī** (wife and husband).
2. **Jānipatī** (wife and husband).

**340. 355. Dhanumh’ ā ca (732).** [Dhanumhā + ā + ca, 3]

**Dhanu**mhā ca **ā**paccayo hoti samāsante.

Gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa so ’yaṃ gāṇḍīvadhanvā.

---

Also there is ‘ā’ after “dhanu”.

At the end of a compound, there is the suffix ‘ā’ after “dhanu”.

---

Examples:

1. Gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa so ’yaṃ **gāṇḍīvadhanvā** (the bow of that one is jointed; he is “gāṇḍīvadhanvā” [one who has a bow with many joints]).

**341. 336. Aṃ vibhattīnam akārantā abyayībhāvā (733).** [Aṃ + vibhattīnaṃ + akārantā + abyayībhāvā, 4]

Tasmā **a**kārantā abyayībhāvasamāsā parāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ kvaci **aṃ** hoti.

Adhicittaṃ (Vin. iii, 278; A. i, 232, 238); yathāvuḍḍhaṃ (JA. i, 234; VinA. i, 10); upakumbhaṃ; yāvajīvaṃ (Vin. i, 27; iii, 63, 133); tiropabbataṃ (D. i, 73; M. i, 41; A. i, 170); tiropākāraṃ (Vin. ii, 348); tirokuṭṭaṃ (D. i, 74; A. i, 170); antopāsādaṃ.

**Kvacī** ti kimatthaṃ? Adhicittassa bhikkhuno.

---

After an adverbial compound that ends in ‘a’, the inflections become ‘aṃ’.

Sometimes, after an adverbial compound that ends in ‘a’, the following inflections become ‘aṃ’.[^21]

---

Examples:

1. **Adhicittaṃ** (higher mind).
2. **Yathāvuḍḍhaṃ** (according to seniority).
3. **Upakumbhaṃ** (the talk that arose near the water pot).
4. **Yāvajīvaṃ** (as long as life lasts).
5. **Tiropabbataṃ** (across the mountain).
6. **Tiropākāraṃ** (across the encircling wall).
7. **Tirokuṭṭaṃ** (beyond the wall).
8. **Antopāsādaṃ** (inside the mansion).

What is the purpose of saying “kvaci” (sometimes)? [To allow exceptions to this sutta, as in:] Of the higher mind (adhicittassa) of the bhikkhu (bhikkhuno).

**342. 337. Saro rasso napuṃsake (734).** [Saro + rasso + napuṃsake, 3]

Napuṃsake vattamānassa abyayībhāvasamāsassa liṅgassa saro rasso hoti.

Kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā iti adhikumāri. Upavadhu; upagaṅgaṃ; upamaṇikaṃ.

---

In the neuter there is a short vowel.

The vowel of the base of the adverbial compound that is in the neuter [gender] is shortened.

---

Examples:

1. Kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā iti **adhikumāri** (the talk that arises referring to the maidens).
2. **Upavadhu** (the talk that arises near the daughter-in-law).
3. **Upagaṅgaṃ** (the talk that arises near the Ganges).
4. **Upamaṇikaṃ** (the talk that arises near the big vessel).

**343. 338. Aññasmā lopo ca (735).** [Aññasmā + lopo + ca, 3]

Aññasmā abyayībhāvasamāsā anakārantā parāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ lopo ca hoti.

Adhitthi (Vism. i, 344), adhikumāri, upavadhu.

---

Also there is elision after others.

Also there is elision of the following inflections after other adverbial compounds not ending in ‘a’.

---

Examples:

1. **Adhitthi** (the talk that arises referring to a woman).
2. **Adhikumāri** (the talk that arises referring to the maidens).
3. **Upavadhu** (the talk that arises near the daughter-in-law).
