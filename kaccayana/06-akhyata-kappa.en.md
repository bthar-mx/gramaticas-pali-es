**6-ĀKHYĀTA-KAPPA**  
**6-VERB CHAPTER**

**PAṬṬHAMA-KAṆḌA**  
**FIRST SECTION**

(K) Ākhyātasāgaram ath’ ajjatanītaraṅgaṃ,   
Dhātujjalaṃ vikaraṇ’-āgama-kālamīnaṃ.   
Lopānubandhariyam atthavibhāgatīraṃ,[^1]   
Dhīrā taranti kavino puthubuddhināvā.

The wise poets by a ship of great knowledge cross the ocean of verbs which has inflections as waves, which has roots as water, which has conjugational signs, augments and tenses as fish, which has elisions and indicatory letters as force, and which has division of meanings as its shore.

(Kh) Vicittasaṅkhāraparikkhitaṃ imaṃ,   
Ākhyātasaddaṃ vipulaṃ asesato.   
Paṇamya sambuddham anantagocaraṃ,   
Sugocaraṃ yaṃ vadato suṇātha me.

Having paid homage to the Buddha who possesses \[a knowledge that has\] infinite sphere and who has a good object (Nibbāna), listen fully to me who is about to speak this vast \[grammar of\] verbs enclosed by variegated formations.

(G) Adhikāre maṅgale c’ eva, nipphanne c’ āvadhāraṇe.   
Anantare c’ apādāne, athasaddo pavattati.

The word “atha” exists \[in these meanings\]: (i) following (adhikāra), (ii) blessing (maṅgala), (iii) completion (nipphanna), (iv) limiting (avadhāraṇa), (v) no-gap (anantara), and (vi) separation (apādāna).

**406\. 429\. Atha[^2] pubbāni vibhattīnaṃ cha parassapadāni (865).** \[Atha \+ pubbāni \+ vibhattīnaṃ \+ cha \+ parassapadāni, 5\]

Atha sabbāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ yāni yāni pubbakāni **cha** padāni, tāni tāni **parassapada**saññāni honti.

Taṃ yathā? **Ti anti, si tha, mi ma.**

**Parassapadam** icc anena kvattho? Kattari parassapadaṃ.

---

The previous six inflections are called “parassapada” (active voice).[^3]

Those that are the previous six of all inflections, they are called “parassapada” (active voice).

---

Which are these?

1. **Ti anti, si tha, mi ma.**

What is the use of the name “parassapada”? For the use of the name “parassapada” in the sutta “Kattari parassapadaṃ” (§456).

---

**407\. 439\. Parāṇy attanopadāni (866).** \[Parāṇi \+ attanopadāni, 2\]

Sabbāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ yāni yāni parāni **cha** padāni, tāni tāni **attanopada**saññāni honti.

Taṃ yathā? **Te ante, se vhe, e mhe.**

**Attanopadam** icc anena kvattho? Attanopadāni bhāve ca kammani.

---

The latest \[six\] are called “attanopada” (middle voice).

Those that are the latest six of all inflections, they are called “attanopada”.

---

Which are these?

1. **Te ante, se vhe, e mhe.**

What is the use of the name “attanopada”? For the use of the name “attanopada” in the sutta “Attanopadāni bhāve ca kammani” (§453).

---

**408\. 431\. Dve dve paṭhama-majjhim’-uttamapurisā (867).** \[Dve-dve \+ paṭhama-majjhima- uttama-purisā, 2\]

Tāsaṃ sabbāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ parassapadānaṃ, attanopadānañ ca **dve dve** padāni **paṭhama-majjhim’-uttamapurisa**saññāni honti.

Taṃ yathā? **Ti anti** iti paṭhamapurisā, **si tha** iti majjhimapurisā, **mi ma** iti uttamapurisā. Attanopadānam pi **te ante** iti paṭhamapurisā, **se vhe** iti majjhimapurisā, **e mhe** iti uttamapurisā. Evaṃ sabbattha.

**Paṭhama-majjhim’-uttamapurisam** icc anena kvattho? Nāmamhi payujjamāne pi tulyādhikaraṇe paṭhamo, tumhe majjhimo, amhe uttamo.

---

The pairs \[are\] third person, second person and first person.[^4]

The group of two words of all inflections of the “parassapada” and “attanopada” is called third person (paṭhamapurisa), second person (majjhimapurisa) and first person (uttamapurisa).

---

Which are these?

1. **Ti anti** are the third person (paṭhamapurisā); **si tha** are the second person (majjhimapurisā); **mi ma** are the first person (uttamapurisā).  
2. Also in the “attanopada”: **te ante** are the third person (paṭhamapurisā); **se vhe** are the second person (majjhimapurisā); **e mhe** are the first person (uttamapurisā).

Thus everywhere.

What is the use of the name “paṭhama-majjhim’-uttamapurisa”? For the use of these names in the suttas “Nāmamhi payujjamāne pi tulyādhikaraṇe paṭhamo”, “Tumhe majjhimo”, “Amhe uttamo” (§410-12).

---

**409\. 441\. Sabbesam ekābhidhāne paro puriso (868).** \[Sabbesaṃ \+ eka-abhidhāne \+ paro \+ puriso, 4\]

Sabbesaṃ tiṇṇaṃ paṭhama-majjhim’-uttamapurisānaṃ ekābhidhāne **paro puriso** gahetabbo.

So ca paṭhati, tvañ ca paṭhasi, **tumhe paṭhatha**. So ca pacati, tvañ ca pacasi, **tumhe pacatha**. 

Evaṃ sesāsu vibhattīsu **paro puriso** yojetabbo.

---

When all \[persons\] are mentioned together, the latter person \[should be taken\].[^5]

Where all three –third person, second person and first person– are mentioned together, the latter person should be taken.

---

Examples:

1. **So ca paṭhati**, **tvañ ca paṭhasi** (he reads and you read): when both persons are combined, **tumhe paṭhatha** (you read).  
2. **So ca pacati**, **tvañ ca pacasi** (he cooks and you cook): when both persons are combined, **tumhe pacatha** (you cook).

Thus in the remaining inflections the latter person should be employed.[^6]

---

**410\. 432\. Nāmamhi payujjamāne pi tulyādhikaraṇe paṭhamo (869).** \[Nāmamhi \+ payujjamāne \+ api \+ tulya-adhikaraṇe \+ paṭhamo, 5\]

**Nāma**mhi payujjamāne pi appayujjamāne pi tulyādhikaraṇe **paṭhamapuriso** hoti.

So gacchati, te gacchanti.

Appayujjamāne pi: Gacchati, gacchanti.

**Tulyādhikaraṇe** ti kimatthaṃ? Tena haññase tvaṃ Devadattena.

---

When used \[or not used\] in a name that has the same locus, \[there is\] the third \[person\].

When used or not used in a name that has the same locus, there is the third person.

---

Examples:

1. **So gacchati** (he goes), **te gacchanti** (they go).

Also when \[the name is\] not used:

1. **Gacchati** (\[he\] goes), **gacchanti** (\[they\] go).

What is the purpose of saying “tulyādhikaraṇe” (that has the same locus)? To prevent the operation of this rule when there is not the same locus, as in the following example: Tena haññase tvaṃ Devadattena (you are killed by that Devadatta).

**411\. 436\. Tumhe majjhimo (870).** \[Tumhe \+ majjhimo, 2\]

**Tumhe** payujjamāne pi appayujjamāne pi tulyādhikaraṇe **majjhimapuriso** hoti.

Tvaṃ yāsi, tumhe yātha.

Appayujjamāne pi: Yāsi, yātha.

**Tulyādhikaraṇe** ti kimatthaṃ? Tayā paccate odano.

---

When “tumha” (you) \[is used or not used\], \[there is\] the second person.

When “tumha” (you) is used or not used with the same locus, there is the second person.

---

Examples:

1. **Tvaṃ yāsi** (you go), **tumhe yātha** (you go).

Also when it is not used:

1. **Yāsi** (\[you\] go), **yātha** (\[you\] go).

What is the purpose of saying “tulyādhikaraṇe” (that has the same locus)? To prevent the operation of this rule when there is not the same locus, as in the following example: Tayā paccate odano (the rice is cooked by you).

---

**412\. 437\. Amhe uttamo (871).** \[Amhe \+ uttamo, 2\]

**Amhe** payujjamāne pi appayujjamāne pi tulyādhikaraṇe **uttamapuriso** hoti.

Ahaṃ yajāmi, mayaṃ yajāma.

Appayujjamāne pi: Yajāmi, yajāma.

**Tulyādhikaraṇe** ti kimatthaṃ? Mayā ijjate Buddho.

---

When “amha” (I) \[is used or not used\], \[there is\] the first person.

When “amha” (I) is used or not used with the same locus, there is the first person.

---

Examples:

1. **Ahaṃ yajāmi** (I sacrifice), **mayaṃ yajāma** (we sacrifice).

Also when it is not used:

1. **Yajāmi** (\[I\] sacrifice), **yajāma** (\[we\] sacrifice).

What is the purpose of saying “tulyādhikaraṇe” (that has the same locus)? To prevent the operation of this rule when there is not the same locus, as in the following example: Mayā ijjate Buddho (the Buddha is honored by me).

**418\. 456\. Hiyyopabhuti[^9] paccakkhe hiyyattanī (886).** \[Hiyyopabhuti \+ paccakkhe \+ hiyyattanī, 3\]

**420\. 471\. Māyoge sabbakāle ca (888).** \[Māyoge \+ sabbakāle \+ ca, 3\]

**Hiyyattanī-ajjatanī** icc etā vibhattiyo yadā **māyogā**,[^12] tadā **sabbakāle** ca honti.

Mā gamā, mā vacā, mā gamī, mā vacī.

**Caggahaṇena** pañcamīvibhatti pi hoti. Mā gacchāhi.

---

In conjunction with “mā”, in all time also.

When \[they are\] in conjunction with “mā”, the inflections “hiyyattanī” and “ajjatanī” are also \[employed\] in all time.

---

Examples:

1. **Mā gamā** (do not go), **mā vacā** (do not talk); **mā gamī** (do not go), **mā vacī** (do not talk).

By taking “ca”, there is also the inflection “pañcamī”:

1. **Mā gacchāhi** (do not go).

**422\. 475\. Kriyātipanne ’tīte kālātipatti (895).** \[Kiriyā-atipanne \+ atīte \+ kālātipatti, 3\]
