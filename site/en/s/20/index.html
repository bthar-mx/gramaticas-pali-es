20. 27. Do dhassa ca (50)

[Do + dhassa + ca = 3 words]

Dha icc etassa sare pare kvaci (sometimes) dakārādeso hoti.

Ekam idāhaṃ bhikkhave samayaṃ (M. i, 401).

Kvacī ti kasmā? Idh’ eva maraṇaṃ bhavissati (Vin. i, 15).

Also ‘da’ of ‘dha’.
When a vowel follows, sometimes ‘dha’ also is substituted by ‘da’.
Example: “Ekam idāhaṃ bhikkhave samayaṃ”.
Example [with formation sequence]
Idāhaṃ
Sequence
  1. idha ahaṃ
  2. idh a ahaṃ (§10)
  3. idh ahaṃ (§12)
  4. ida ahaṃ (§20)
  5. id a ahaṃ (§10)
  6. id ahaṃ (§12)
  7. id āhaṃ (§15)
  8. idāhaṃ (§11)

Why say “sometimes”? [To prevent the operation of this rule in the following example] “Idh’ eva maraṇaṃ bhavissati”.

Counter-example
Idh’ eva
Sequence
  1. idha eva
  2. idh a eva (§10)
  3. idh eva (§12)
  4. idheva (§11)
  5. idh’ eva (ME)
Extension by “ca” (accumulating):

Caggahaṇena dhakārassa hakārādeso hoti. Sāhu dassanam ariyānaṃ (Khu. i, 34).

By taking “ca”, there is also substitution of ‘dha’ by ‘ha’, [such as in] “sāhu dassanam ariyānaṃ”.

Example
Sāhu
Sequence
  1. sādhu
  2. sā dhu (§10)
  3. sāhu (§20)
Extension by division of the sutta (suttavibhāgena):

Suttavibhāgena bahudhā siyā: To dassa, yathā? Sugato (Vin. i, 1). Ṭo tassa, yathā? Dukkaṭaṃ (Vin. v, 262). Dho tassa, yathā? Gandhabbo (M. i, 332). Tro ttassa, yathā? Atrajo (Vin. iii, 428). Ko gassa, yathā? Kulūpako (Vin. i, 192). Lo rassa, yathā? Mahāsālo (D. ii, 121). Jo yassa, yathā? Gavajo (Khu. v, 283). Bbo vvassa, yathā? Kubbato (Khu. v, 95). Ko yassa, yathā? Sake (Khu. vi, 325). Yo jassa, yathā? Niyaṃ puttaṃ (Khu. i, 11, 301). Ko tassa, yathā? Niyako. Cco ttassa, yathā? Bhacco. Pho passa, yathā? Nipphatti. Kho kassa, yathā? Nikkhamati. Icc evamādī yojetabbā.

By dividing the sutta [into similar rules], there are many applications: ‘d’ is changed to ‘t’ as in “Sugato” (Sugado → Sugato); ‘t’ to ‘ṭ’ as in “dukkaṭaṃ” (dukkataṃ → dukkaṭaṃ); ‘t’ to ‘dh’ as in “gandhabbo” (gantabbo → gandhabbo); ‘tt’ to ‘tr’ as in “atrajo” (attajo → atrajo); ‘g’ to ‘k’ as in “kulūpako” (kulūpago → kulūpako); ‘r’ to ‘l’ as in “mahāsālo” (mahāsāro → mahāsālo); ‘y’ to ‘j’ as in “gavajo” (gavayo → gavajo); ‘vv’ to ‘bb’ as in “kubbato” (kuvvato → kubbato); ‘y’ to ‘k’ as in “sake” (saye → sake); ‘j’ to ‘y’ as in “niyaṃ” (nijaṃ → niyaṃ); ‘t’ to ‘k’ as in “niyako” (niyato → niyako); ‘tt’ to ‘cc’ as in “bhacco” (bhatto → bhacco); ‘p’ to ‘ph’ as in “nipphatti” (nippatti → nipphatti); ‘k’ to ‘kh’ as in “nikkhamati” (nikkamati → nikkhamati). Other examples beginning with these should be formed.
